\begin{letter}{The Editors\\Artificial Intelligence in Medicine}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit the research article ``Separating decision-rule failures from representation failures
in external validation of diabetic retinopathy grading models'' for consideration in
\emph{Artificial Intelligence in Medicine}.

When a diabetic retinopathy grading model fails on a new population, the failure is usually
attributed to the learned representation and answered by changing the model. Ordinal grading
models, however, convert scores into grades with a decision rule that is rarely examined. Across ten
development models, four external populations, the reverse direction of transfer, two models
fine-tuned on \pn{eye.eb3.trainn} EyePACS images and five training seeds, we show that the ranking
of images largely transferred while the mapping from score to grade did not: the same models
over-graded one population and under-graded another, so that a referral threshold fitted for 85\%
sensitivity reached \pn{aptos.tr.e08.B.sens}\% on one and \pn{ddr.tr.e08.B.sens}\% on the other.
The same threshold fitted on EyePACS over-referred everywhere, which shows that the direction of the
shift belongs to the population and the sign of a transferred threshold's error to the development
labels. Refitting cut-points recovered part of the loss, but only where ranking survived and only
with local labels; label-free adaptation did not help.

We believe the work suits the journal for three reasons. It contributes evaluation methodology for
medical AI: a decomposition of external performance into per-threshold discrimination and the
score-to-grade mapping, and a matched decision rule for comparing models, with its record of changed
and unchanged conclusions, including a published model whose reported advantage did not survive it.
It gives a deployment rule that holds for any model: the risk that a locally set referral threshold
misses its sensitivity floor depends on the number of referable cases labelled, follows a
distribution-free form, and translates into images at any screening prevalence. And it is
transparent about its own evidence: the analysis plans were committed to a public repository before
the corresponding analyses, every outcome is reported with its verdict, falsified ones included,
post-hoc analyses are labelled, and every number in the manuscript is generated from archived
per-image predictions that are public with the code.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Parts of the
development work form the basis of a Master's thesis at the University of Tehran. All data are public
and de-identified. The authors declare no competing interests. The use of an AI assistant for code,
analysis runs and text is described in the Methods and in the generative-AI declaration.

\closing{Sincerely,}

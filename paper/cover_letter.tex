\begin{letter}{The Editors\\Computers in Biology and Medicine}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit the Full Length Article ``Separating decision-rule failures from representation failures
in external validation of diabetic retinopathy grading models'' for consideration in
\emph{Computers in Biology and Medicine}, section Foundations of Artificial Intelligence in Medicine.

When a diabetic retinopathy grading model fails on a new population, the failure is usually
attributed to the learned representation and answered by changing the model. Ordinal grading
models, however, convert scores into grades with a decision rule that is rarely examined. Across ten
development models, four external populations (APTOS 2019, DDR, DeepDRiD and SUSTech-SYSU), the
reverse direction of transfer, two models trained on about 28\,000 images and five training seeds,
we show that the ranking of images transferred while the mapping from score to grade did not: the
same models over-graded one population and under-graded another, so that a referral threshold
fitted for 85\% sensitivity reached \pn{aptos.tr.e08.B.sens}\% on one and \pn{ddr.tr.e08.B.sens}\% on
the other. Refitting cut-points alone recovered part of the loss, but only where ranking survived and
only with local labels; label-free adaptation did not help. We report with equal weight that two of
our own predictions were falsified.

We believe the work suits the journal for three reasons. It contributes evaluation methodology for
medical AI: a decomposition of external performance, and a matched decision rule for comparing
models, with its record of changed and unchanged conclusions, including a published model whose
reported advantage did not survive it. It is directly usable in deployment: we quantify how many
locally labelled images a referral threshold needs and how often that procedure fails, and translate
the operating point into missed referrals per 10\,000 people screened. And it is reproducible: the
analysis plans were committed to a public repository before the corresponding analyses, every
number in the manuscript is generated from archived per-image predictions, and code, splits and
predictions are public.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Parts of the
development work form the basis of a Master's thesis at the University of Tehran. All data are public
and de-identified. The authors declare no competing interests. AI-assisted coding and editing are
disclosed in the Methods and in the generative-AI declaration.

\closing{Sincerely,}

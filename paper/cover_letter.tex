\begin{letter}{The Editors\\npj Digital Medicine}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit the Article ``Decision rules rather than representations drive external failures of
diabetic retinopathy grading models'' for consideration in \emph{npj Digital Medicine}.

When a diabetic retinopathy grading model fails on a new population, the field attributes the
failure to the learned representation and responds by changing the model: a larger backbone, a
foundation encoder, a new ordinal loss. We show that a large part of what is reported as model
failure, and as model difference, is instead produced by the decision rule that turns the
model's ordinal outputs into a grade. Across development models, five training seeds, two
external populations (APTOS 2019 and DDR) and the reverse direction of transfer, the mild
grade collapses under the shipped rule and is restored by refitting four cut-points alone,
without retraining. Development-fitted thresholds overshoot their target on new populations,
and agreement statistics cannot see the referral operating point. Reanalysing the released
predictions of a published ordinal model reproduces its result and the same pattern. Of nine
pre-registered interventions on the model, \pn{iv.nfalsified} were falsified at matched
calibration, including a retinal foundation model.

We think the work suits \emph{npj Digital Medicine} for three reasons. First, its message is
practical for deployment: before a grading model is replaced, its decision rule should be
recalibrated and evaluated, and we quantify how many locally labelled images that requires and
how often it does harm. Second, it corrects how model comparisons are reported: we propose
\emph{matched calibration} as a reporting rule and document its record, including a case in
which our own earlier archived result did not survive regeneration from per-image predictions.
Third, the study is fully reproducible: every outcome was pre-registered in a public
repository before the corresponding run, every number in the manuscript is generated from
archived per-image predictions, and code, splits and predictions are public.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All data are
public and de-identified. The authors declare no competing interests. The use of an AI
assistant in analysis and drafting is disclosed in Methods.

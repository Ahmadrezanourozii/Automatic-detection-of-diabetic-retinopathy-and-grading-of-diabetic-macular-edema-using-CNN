\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig1_design.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Study design.} Development models were trained on a pooled IDRiD and Messidor-2
set under a frozen 5-fold split (after pretraining on EyePACS) and evaluated without further tuning
on four external populations; a model trained on APTOS was evaluated in the reverse direction, and
two models were fine-tuned on \pn{eye.eb3.trainn} EyePACS images. Every model's ordinal outputs
were decoded under the same decision rules: R1, the shipped default; R2 and R3, fitted on
development predictions; R4 and R4m, fitted on external labels by two-fold cross-fitting
(diagnostic only); and R5 and R5b, label-free adaptation to the external population, with R0a, the
unadapted argmax, as their reference.}
\label{fig:design}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figures/fig3_transfer.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Referral thresholds fitted on development data do not hold their operating point.}
A threshold on $P(\mathrm{grade}\geq2)$ was fitted on the reference model's development out-of-fold
predictions for each target sensitivity (x-axis) and applied unchanged to each external
population; points show the achieved sensitivity with 95\% bootstrap intervals, and the diagonal is
perfect transfer. Targets: 80\%; 85\% (the superiority endpoint of the first autonomous pivotal
trial); 87.2\% (that trial's achieved sensitivity); 95.5\%. The same thresholds over-refer where the
models over-grade (APTOS, SUSTech-SYSU), under-refer where they under-grade most (DDR) and transfer
where the shift is small (DeepDRiD).}
\label{fig:transfer}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig2_rules.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{External mild-grade recall and referral sensitivity of every development model
under every decision rule.} Rows are models (Table~\ref{tab:models}) ordered by development QWK;
markers show the same model under four rules. \textbf{a},\textbf{b}, APTOS 2019; \textbf{c},\textbf{d},
DDR. On APTOS, mild recall at the shipped rule (R1) is low in every model and is restored by
cut-points refitted on external labels (R4m, diagnostic) at the cost of referral sensitivity, because
mild DR is not referable; rules fitted on development data (R2, R3) do not restore it. On DDR the mild
grade is not collapsed and no rule recovers much. Models differ at a fixed rule by more than the
deployable rules differ within a model (pre-specified outcome O7 falsified).}
\label{fig:rules}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[p]

\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig5_samplesize.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{How many local labels a referral threshold needs.} The threshold is set to the
largest value giving at least 85\% sensitivity on the referable cases of a labelled sample; harm is
a population sensitivity below 80\%. \textbf{a}, risk of harm against the number of referable cases
in the sample: closed form (line), which holds for any model and population with continuous scores,
and simulation for the selected model on each external set (markers; 2\,000 draws, sensitivity
measured on the referable images not drawn; post hoc). \textbf{b}, risk against the number of
consecutive images labelled at referable prevalences of 5, 10 and 20\% (closed form) and at the
\pn{ph.prev.min}--\pn{ph.prev.max}\% of the external sets as sampled (dashed), with the pre-specified
simulation for the selected model (markers; 500 draws, sensitivity measured on the remaining images,
which adds evaluation noise at large $n$ on the smaller sets). Dotted lines, 10\% and 5\% risk.}
\label{fig:samplesize}
\end{figure}

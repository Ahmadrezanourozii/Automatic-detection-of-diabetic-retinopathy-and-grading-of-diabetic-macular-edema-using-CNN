\section{Materials and methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Study design and analysis plans}

This is a retrospective modelling study on public, de-identified fundus photographs. An
evaluation protocol (unit of independence, held-out sets, model selection on development data
only, metrics and uncertainty) was fixed before the first training run. For the analyses
reported here, the plan --- hypothesis, control, analysis and the result that would falsify
it --- was committed to the public repository before the analysis was run: the derivation test
of the macular-oedema grade (Supplementary Note~2), nine first-stage outcomes O1--O9 (seed
replicates, reverse direction, second external corpus, five-fold foundation model) and
second-stage outcomes A1--E2 (mechanism, label-free rules, sample size, two further external sets
and the models trained on EyePACS), written after an internal pre-submission review of an earlier
draft. These are analysis plans committed to version control, not entries in a public registry;
their commit identifiers and times are in Supplementary Table~S1, and every outcome with its
prediction, falsifying result and verdict is in Supplementary Table~S2. Two limits apply. The
repository history begins on 31 August 2026, so the pre-specification of development runs trained
before that date cannot be verified, and we call those models pre-specified only in the sense that
the set of ten was fixed in the first-stage plan. And the second-stage prediction A1 was written
knowing the overall direction of the shift on APTOS and DDR; what it added was the grade-by-grade
form, and DeepDRiD and SUSTech-SYSU, for which no direction was known or predicted, were its first
test on new populations. Analyses added after an internal editorial assessment of the manuscript
are labelled \emph{post hoc}. Reporting follows TRIPOD+AI\cite{collins2024tripodai} and
CLAIM\cite{tejani2024claim} (Supplementary Table~S3).

\subsection{Data}

The \emph{development pool} combined IDRiD\cite{porwal2018idrid,porwal2020challenge}
(\pn{n.idrid} images) and Messidor-2\cite{decenciere2014messidor,abramoff2013messidor2}
(\pn{n.messidor2} gradable images with the adjudicated grades of Krause \emph{et
al.}\cite{krause2018grader}), \pn{n.dev} images in total (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts};
Fig.~\ref{fig:design}). Messidor-2 images were taken from a public mirror of the original release
preprocessed to 512$\times$512 pixels; the two higher-resolution models (560 and 640 pixels) used a
mirror at the original 2240$\times$1488 pixels that covers 1\,057 of the 1\,744 images
(Supplementary Table~S4). The external sets, never used for training, model selection or threshold
fitting, were APTOS 2019 (\pn{n.aptos} images, India, single grader), DDR\cite{li2019ddr}
(\pn{n.ddr} gradable images, 147 hospitals in China, seven graders; used from a public 512-pixel
mirror), the regular fundus images of DeepDRiD\cite{liu2022deepdrid} (\pn{r2.deepdrid.n} images of
500 patients from three Chinese diabetes screening programmes) and SUSTech-SYSU\cite{lin2020sustech}
(\pn{r2.sustech.n} graded images; the 68 images of laser scars carry no grade). SUSTech-SYSU is a
case--control collection: its grade-0 images are of healthy volunteers without diabetes, photographed
at routine health examinations, and its DR images were graded by two ophthalmologists with
adjudication by a third\cite{lin2020sustech}. In the reverse direction APTOS was the training corpus
and Messidor-2 and IDRiD were external. The EyePACS 2015 training set (\pn{n.eyepacs} images,
single grader) was used to pretrain the development models and, in the second stage, as the
development set of two further models.
All corpora use the international 5-grade scale\cite{wilkinson2003scale}; referable DR is grade
$\geq$2 and, because macular oedema is not graded in the external sets, it is defined by DR
grade alone. A content audit (256-bit difference hash after the training crop) checked every
development and EyePACS image against every external image (Supplementary Note~7).
Patient identifiers are published only for DeepDRiD and EyePACS; Messidor-2 is distributed as
pairs of images per examination, but the pairing file is not part of the mirrors used, so
resampling elsewhere is by image (near-duplicates merged).

\subsection{Models}

All models share one design: a backbone, global average pooling and ordinal threshold heads
that output four logits for $P(y>k)$\cite{cao2020rank}, trained with masked binary
cross-entropy, a grade-balanced sampler, AdamW, cosine decay, weight averaging and
dihedral test-time augmentation (details in Supplementary Table~S4). The reference model is a
DenseNet121\cite{huang2017densenet} pretrained on ImageNet and then EyePACS and fine-tuned at
448 pixels; the selected model replaces the backbone with EfficientNet-B3\cite{tan2019efficientnet}.
The other eight pre-specified development models change one factor each
(Table~\ref{tab:models}): resolution (224, 560 and native 640 pixels), a macula-centred crop,
linear-probe-then-fine-tune\cite{kumar2022lpft}, CORAL and CORN heads\cite{cao2020rank,shi2023corn}
and ConvNeXt-tiny\cite{liu2022convnext}; the retinal foundation model RETFound\cite{zhou2023retfound}
was fine-tuned separately. Development results are out of fold over a frozen 5-fold split
stratified by corpus and grade; external predictions average the five fold models. The development
models had seen EyePACS during pretraining; what the two EyePACS models (DenseNet121 and
EfficientNet-B3, 448 pixels, 15 epochs from ImageNet) changed was the final training set and its
labels. They were trained on four folds of a patient-grouped split of EyePACS
(\pn{eye.eb3.trainn} images) and used the fifth (\pn{eye.eb3.devn} images) both to select the
checkpoint and to fit development rules, so their development estimates are single-fold and
slightly optimistic.

\subsection{Decision rules and the matched decision rule}

For an ordinal model with $p_k=P(y>k)$ the expected grade is $s=\sum_k p_k$. Every model's
outputs were decoded under the same rules (Supplementary Note~1). \textbf{R1} is the shipped
rule: the number of leading thresholds with $p_k>0.5$. \textbf{R2} places four cut-points on $s$
to maximise QWK on development out-of-fold predictions, and \textbf{R3} moves R2's grade-1/2
cut to give 90\% development referable sensitivity. \textbf{R4} and \textbf{R4m} fit the
cut-points on the external labels, maximising QWK or macro-averaged recall, by two-fold
cross-fitting; they are diagnostics of what local labels could recover, never performance.
\textbf{R5} adapts the class posteriors (derived from the ordinal outputs) to the external
population without labels by EM estimation of the target prior\cite{saerens2002adjusting},
and \textbf{R5b} by black-box shift estimation\cite{lipton2018bbse}; both decode by argmax, so
their like-for-like reference is \textbf{R0a}, the argmax of the unadapted posteriors.

The \emph{matched decision rule} is the comparison rule used throughout: no difference between
two models is attributed to the representation unless it survives refitting both models'
cut-points by the same cross-fitted procedure (on development data, fold $f$'s cut-points come
from the other four folds; externally, R4). Because a per-grade verdict can depend on how the
cut-points are cross-fitted (Supplementary Note~3), every matched claim names its procedure.

\subsection{Decomposing external performance}

Discrimination was measured at every ordinal threshold (area under the ROC curve of $p_k$ for
$y>k$, $k=0,\dots,3$) and overall by Harrell's C of $s$. The mapping from score to grade was
described by the mean expected grade given the true grade, $\mathrm{E}[s\mid y=k]$; under a pure
prior (label) shift this quantity is the same in every population, so a grade-by-grade departure
from its development value indicates a conditional shift (outcome A1). To test whether image
quality alone can produce the under-grading seen on DDR (A2), the development pool was re-scored
out of fold after five fixed degradations of the cached image (JPEG quality 30 at 512 pixels;
downsampling to 256 pixels; Gaussian blur, $\sigma=2$ pixels; gamma 1.8 darkening; contrast
reduced to 60\%), and per-corpus image statistics were computed (A3).

\subsection{Threshold transfer and local recalibration}

A referral threshold on $P(y\geq2)$ was fitted on development predictions for target
sensitivities of 80\%, 85\% (the superiority endpoint of the first autonomous pivotal
trial\cite{abramoff2018pivotal}), 87.2\% and 95.5\%, and applied unchanged externally.

Local recalibration was sized with the operational objective. In the pre-specified analysis (C),
$n$ external images ($n=25$--800, 500 draws each) were drawn at random, the referral threshold was
set to the largest value giving at least 85\% sensitivity on those $n$, and sensitivity was measured
on the remaining images; a draw counts as \emph{harm} when that sensitivity falls below 80\%. Because
this threshold depends only on the referable images in the sample, and the external sets are
enriched (40--45\% referable), the analysis was repeated post hoc in terms of the number $k$ of
referable cases labelled ($k=20$--300, 2\,000 draws), with the threshold's sensitivity measured on
all of the corpus's referable images not drawn. With continuous scores the population sensitivity of
the cut at the $j$-th largest of $k$ referable scores ($j=\lceil0.85k\rceil$) follows a
Beta$(j,\,k-j+1)$ distribution whatever the model or population, which gives the risk of harm in
closed form; for $n$ consecutive images at referable prevalence $\pi$ the number of referable cases
is binomial$(n,\pi)$. Positive and negative predictive values and the number of referable patients
missed per 10\,000 screened were derived at referable prevalences of 5, 10 and 20\%.

\subsection{Statistics}

The primary grading metric is quadratic weighted kappa (QWK)\cite{cohen1968kappa}; the
operationally primary pair is referable sensitivity and specificity, never quoted alone.
Per-grade recall, precision and F1, Cohen's $\kappa$, macro-averaged recall (a diagnostic and the
objective of R4m, never a deployment objective), area under the curve, Brier score and expected
calibration error are reported
beside them. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from 2\,000 bootstrap resamples of groups
(patients where identified, otherwise images); differences between models are paired bootstraps,
and an interval containing zero is reported as indistinguishable, not ranked. Model rankings were
compared with Kendall's $\tau$, with a two-sided permutation $p$-value (10\,000 permutations) and,
post hoc, a 95\% interval from 1\,000 image resamples. Because case mix alone can raise an area
under the curve, the referral AUC was also computed post hoc with each image weighted to the
development grade mix (within referable and within non-referable images), beside the AUC between
adjacent grades. A difference between configurations is treated as beyond training
noise only if it exceeds $2\sqrt{2}$ standard deviations of matched development QWK across five
seeds of the reference model. No correction for multiplicity is applied; per-grade claims are
treated as credible only when they replicate across conditions.

\subsection{Software and reproducibility}

Every number in the article is generated from archived per-image predictions into a ledger from
which the manuscript is compiled; no number is typed by hand. An AI assistant (Claude, Anthropic)
was used under the authors' direction to write analysis and training code, to run the analyses, to
submit the training and inference jobs to Kaggle and to draft text. The authors directed the work
and its interpretation, reviewed the code and its outputs, verified the reported results against
the archived predictions and take responsibility for them. Code, splits, analysis plans and
predictions are public (Data and code availability).

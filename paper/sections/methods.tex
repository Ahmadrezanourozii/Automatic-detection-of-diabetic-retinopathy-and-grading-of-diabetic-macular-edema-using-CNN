\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection*{Study design and pre-registration}

This is a retrospective modelling study on public, de-identified fundus photographs. It was
conducted under an evaluation protocol frozen before the first training run (unit of
independence, held-out sets, model selection on the development pool only, metrics and
uncertainty), and every experiment was pre-registered: its hypothesis, control and falsifying
outcome were committed to the version-controlled repository before the run was launched, and
the commit history is the record\cite{nosek2018prereg}. The nine interventions on the model
and the nine outcomes O1--O9 of the journal experiments (seed replicates, the reverse
direction, the second external corpus and the foundation-model completion) were registered in
this way; a training run cannot be launched by our kernel builder without a recorded
hypothesis. Reporting follows TRIPOD+AI\cite{collins2024tripodai} and
CLAIM\cite{tejani2024claim} where applicable (Supplementary Table~S1).

\subsection*{Datasets}

The \emph{development pool} combined IDRiD\cite{porwal2018idrid,porwal2020challenge}
(\pn{n.idrid} images; 5-grade International Clinical DR scale\cite{wilkinson2003scale}
and a 3-grade risk-of-DME label defined by the distance of the nearest hard exudate to the
macula) and Messidor-2\cite{decenciere2014messidor,abramoff2013messidor2}
(\pn{n.messidor2} gradable images with adjudicated DR grades and a binary referable-DME
label\cite{krause2018grader}), \pn{n.dev} images in total (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}). IDRiD's
official train/test partition was pooled, because a 103-image test split cannot resolve the
effects studied here (half-width of a 95\% interval on accuracy $\approx$6 points). The
\emph{external} DR sets were APTOS 2019 (\pn{n.aptos} images from Aravind Eye Hospital, India;
single-grader labels) and DDR\cite{li2019ddr} (\pn{n.ddr} gradable images from 147 hospitals
in 23 Chinese provinces, graded by seven graders; images distributed at 512 pixels). Neither
was used for training, model selection or threshold fitting, and both were held out from the
first run. The EyePACS 2015 training set (\pn{n.eyepacs} images, single-grader labels) was used
only to pretrain the DR head. In the reverse-direction experiment APTOS was the training
corpus and Messidor-2 and IDRiD the external sets. Per-image identity checks found no image of
any external set in any training set. No patient identifiers are published for IDRiD,
Messidor-2 or APTOS; the unit of resampling is therefore the image, and near-duplicate images
were merged into one group by a 256-bit difference hash calibrated on fundus images (distinct
pairs $\geq$28 bits apart, re-encodings of one image $\leq$8; threshold 16).

\subsection*{Labels}

DR labels are the 5-grade international scale in all corpora (0 no DR, 1 mild, 2 moderate,
3 severe non-proliferative, 4 proliferative); referable DR is grade $\geq$2. DME is graded
0--2 in IDRiD (none; exudates more than one disc diameter from the macula; within one disc
diameter). Messidor-2's binary referable-DME label is, by its definition, exactly IDRiD's
grade 2; a non-referable Messidor-2 image is grade 0 \emph{or} 1. In threshold form this
partial label supervises the second DME threshold and leaves the first unsupervised for that
image, so no label is imputed. The 3-grade DME metrics are computed on IDRiD only
(\pn{n.idrid} images, \pn{dme.n1} of them in the middle grade), always beside the
majority-class floor (\pn{dme.floor}\%), and without gating on DR.

\subsection*{Model and training}

All models share one design: a convolutional (or transformer) backbone, global average
pooling, and two ordinal threshold heads (hidden width 256, dropout 0.4) that output
$K-1$ logits for $P(y>k)$, four for DR and two for DME\cite{cao2020rank}. The loss is
$0.6\,\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{DR}+0.4\,\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{DME}$, each a masked binary
cross-entropy over the thresholds with label smoothing 0.05. Images were cropped to the
retinal disc, cached at a long side of 560 pixels and resized to the training resolution
(448 pixels unless stated), with unrestricted rotation, scaling (0.88--1.12), translation
($\pm$4\%), horizontal and vertical flips and mild photometric jitter; a sampler balanced the
DR grades. Training used AdamW (backbone learning rate $10^{-4}$, heads $10^{-3}$, weight
decay $10^{-4}$), a 5\% linear warm-up followed by cosine decay to 2\% of the peak, gradient
clipping at 1.0, mixed precision, and an exponential moving average of the weights (decay
0.999) for evaluation. The reference configuration (E08) is DenseNet121\cite{huang2017densenet}
pretrained on ImageNet, then on EyePACS for 5 epochs, then fine-tuned for 40 epochs with batch
16. The selected model (E11FULL) replaces the backbone with EfficientNet-B3\cite{tan2019efficientnet}
(30 epochs). Every other configuration changes exactly one factor relative to its control
(Supplementary Table~S2): resolution (224, 560 and native 640 pixels), a macula-centred crop
for the DME head driven by a fovea localiser validated before use (median error
\pn{fovea.median} disc diameters), linear-probe-then-fine-tune\cite{kumar2022lpft}, CORAL and
CORN ordinal heads\cite{cao2020rank,shi2023corn}, ConvNeXt-tiny\cite{liu2022convnext}, and the
RETFound colour-fundus ViT-L/16 encoder\cite{zhou2023retfound} (224 pixels, linear probe for 5
epochs then fine-tuning at $10^{-5}$). At the end of each fold the epoch with the highest
$0.6\,\mathrm{QWK}_\mathrm{DR}+0.4\,\mathrm{QWK}_\mathrm{DME}$ on the held-out fold was kept,
and its predictions were averaged over the four dihedral flips (test-time augmentation).
External predictions average the logits of the five fold models.

\subsection*{Cross-validation and model selection}

All development results are out-of-fold over the full pool: a 5-fold split, stratified by
corpus and DR grade and grouped by near-duplicate cluster, was built once, fingerprinted and
committed before the first run, and every model loads it from that file. Configurations were
ranked by development QWK at matched calibration (below) and the selected model was declared
in the repository before any external number was examined; no external result was used to
choose a model, a threshold or a hyper-parameter. Because the checkpoint epoch was chosen on
the held-out fold, development estimates carry a small selection optimism. We estimated it by
re-selecting each fold's epoch from the \emph{other} folds' validation curves: across nine
5-fold runs the median optimism was \pn{optimism.median} DR QWK (range \pn{optimism.min} to
\pn{optimism.max}). External estimates are not affected.

\subsection*{Decision rules and matched calibration}

For an ordinal model with threshold probabilities $p_k=P(y>k)$ the expected grade is
$s=\sum_k p_k$. We compare five decision rules, fixed before the journal experiments.
\textbf{R1} is the shipped rule: grade $=$ the number of leading thresholds with $p_k>0.5$
(made rank-consistent with a cumulative minimum). \textbf{R2} places four cut-points on $s$ to
maximise QWK on the development out-of-fold predictions. \textbf{R3} is R2 with the grade-1/2
cut moved to the most specific value that gives a development referable sensitivity of at
least 0.90. R1--R3 are deployable: none uses external labels. \textbf{R4} and \textbf{R4m}
fit the cut-points on the external labels themselves, maximising QWK or macro-averaged recall,
by two-fold cross-fitting (each half's cut-points are fitted on the other half); they are
diagnostic upper bounds on what recalibration with local labels can recover and are never
reported as performance. Cut-points are found by coordinate ascent (eight rounds; 60
candidates per cut on development data and 80 on external data, reproducing the archived
analyses exactly; the grid alone moves a matched QWK by at most \pn{grid.effect}).

\emph{Matched calibration} is the comparison rule applied throughout: no difference between
two models is attributed to representation unless it survives refitting both models' cut-points
by the same cross-fitted procedure (on the development pool, fold $f$'s cut-points come from
the other four folds). The rule is only evidence if it can return either answer; its record
in this study is reported in Table~\ref{tab:record}.

\subsection*{Metrics and statistical analysis}

The primary grading metric is QWK\cite{cohen1968kappa}; the operationally primary pair is
referable-DR sensitivity and specificity, never quoted alone; per-grade recall is reported
beside every headline, and macro-averaged recall is used as a diagnostic, never as an
objective (optimising it costs referral sensitivity; see Results). We also report Cohen's
unweighted $\kappa$, accuracy and macro-F1, and, for the referral probability
$P(\mathrm{grade}\geq2)=p_1$, the area under the ROC curve, the Brier score and the expected
calibration error (ECE, ten equal-width bins) together, since ECE alone can reward an
uninformative predictor\cite{poyrazer2026calibration}. Intervals are 95\% percentile
intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of groups; differences between models are paired
bootstraps over the same groups, and a difference whose interval contains zero is reported as
indistinguishable, not ranked. No correction for multiple comparisons is applied; per-grade
claims are therefore treated as credible only when they replicate across conditions.

\subsection*{Recalibration sample size}

To quantify how many locally labelled images recalibration needs, we drew $n\in\{25, 50, 100,
200, 400\}$ external images at random (200 draws per size), fitted cut-points on those $n$
alone (maximising macro-recall), and evaluated on the remaining images, so that every estimate
is out of sample with respect to its cut-points, as in deployment. The probability of harm is
the fraction of draws in which macro-recall fell below that of the shipped rule on the same
held-out images.

\subsection*{Seed replicates, reverse direction and second external corpus}

Seeds 1--4 of the reference configuration were trained with every other setting identical
(E08 is seed 0; consumption manifests confirm the same files were read), and each was scored
on APTOS and DDR. The reverse-direction model used the reference recipe trained on APTOS with
a 5-fold split built by the same code before training (near-duplicates merged), and was scored
on Messidor-2 and IDRiD. The archived development models were re-scored on DDR (and those
without archived APTOS predictions on APTOS) from their stored fold weights, with the same
inference recipe.

\subsection*{Provenance controls}

Each run records, from inside the process that read the disk, a consumption manifest (number of
images per corpus and a hash over the resolved paths); runs whose manifests differ cannot be
combined or compared without the difference being printed into the output. Every number in
this article is generated from archived artefacts into a ledger, and the manuscript is compiled
from that ledger, so no number in the text is typed by hand.

\subsection*{Use of AI assistance}

An AI system (Claude, Anthropic) assisted with analysis code, the execution of the
pre-registered journal experiments and drafting of the manuscript under the direction of the
authors, who verified the analyses, the numbers against their generated sources, and every
cited claim against its primary source, and take full responsibility for the content.

\subsection*{Data availability}

All images are from public datasets: IDRiD (IEEE DataPort), Messidor-2 (ADCIS, with
adjudicated grades from Krause \emph{et al.}\cite{krause2018grader}), APTOS 2019 and EyePACS
2015 (Kaggle) and DDR\cite{li2019ddr}. Per-image out-of-fold and external predictions for every
model, the frozen split, the pre-registration records and every generated table are available
in the project repository
(\url{https://github.com/Ahmadrezanourozii/Automatic-detection-of-diabetic-retinopathy-and-grading-of-diabetic-macular-edema-using-CNN}).

\subsection*{Code availability}

All code (training, evaluation, decision-rule analyses, figure and number generation) is
available in the same repository; each run is reproducible from the commit hash recorded in its
log.

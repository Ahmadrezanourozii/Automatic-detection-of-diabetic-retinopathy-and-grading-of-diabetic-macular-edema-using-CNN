\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Cohorts and models}

The ten pre-specified development models reached a matched development QWK between
\pn{dev.e21cnxa.matched} and \pn{dev.e11full.matched}; the selected EfficientNet-B3, chosen on
development data before any external number was examined, reached \pn{sel.dev.qwk}
(Table~\ref{tab:models}). The four external populations differed in their grade distributions
(Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}): referable DR made up \pn{r2.aptos.prevref}\% of APTOS,
\pn{r2.ddr.prevref}\% of DDR, \pn{r2.deepdrid.prevref}\% of DeepDRiD and
\pn{r2.sustech.prevref}\% of SUSTech-SYSU.

\subsection{Ranking largely transferred to new populations; the mapping from score to grade did not}

Discrimination largely survived the change of population (Fig.~\ref{fig:shift}a). Across the
ten development models the area under the ROC curve for referable disease, $P(y>1)$, was
\pn{r2.aptos.auc1.min}--\pn{r2.aptos.auc1.max} on APTOS, \pn{r2.ddr.auc1.min}--\pn{r2.ddr.auc1.max}
on DDR and \pn{r2.deepdrid.auc1.min}--\pn{r2.deepdrid.auc1.max} on DeepDRiD, against
\pn{ph.dev.aucref.min}--\pn{ph.dev.aucref.max} in development; Harrell's C of the expected grade was
\pn{r2.aptos.cindex.min}--\pn{r2.aptos.cindex.max} on APTOS and
\pn{r2.ddr.cindex.min}--\pn{r2.ddr.cindex.max} on DDR, against \pn{r2.dev.e08.cindex} for the
reference model in development. Ranking did not survive everywhere: the proliferative threshold,
weak already in development (\pn{r2.dev.auc3.min}--\pn{r2.dev.auc3.max}), fell to
\pn{r2.aucall.allmin} at worst, and the foundation model's referral ranking degraded on DDR
(\pn{rf.ddr.auroc}; below).

On SUSTech-SYSU the referral area under the curve was \pn{r2.sustech.auc1.min}--\pn{r2.sustech.auc1.max},
higher than in development, which better models cannot explain (Supplementary Table~S12). The set is
a case--control collection whose grade-0 images come from healthy volunteers without diabetes, and
only \pn{ph.sustech.nmild} of its images (\pn{ph.sustech.pctmild}\%) are mild. Weighting its images
to the development grade mix left the area under the curve at
\pn{ph.sustech.aucrefdevmix.min}--\pn{ph.sustech.aucrefdevmix.max}, and no-DR against moderate images
separated almost perfectly (\pn{ph.sustech.auc0v2.min}--\pn{ph.sustech.auc0v2.max}, against
\pn{ph.dev.auc0v2.min}--\pn{ph.dev.auc0v2.max} in development), so the excess lies in how clear-cut
the cases are within grades, not only in the grade mix. APTOS showed a narrower version of the same
pattern: its no-DR and mild images separated far better than in development
(\pn{ph.aptos.auc0v1.min}--\pn{ph.aptos.auc0v1.max} against \pn{ph.dev.auc0v1.min}--\pn{ph.dev.auc0v1.max}),
while its mild and moderate images separated worse (\pn{ph.aptos.auc1v2.min}--\pn{ph.aptos.auc1v2.max}).
%%ACQ%%
We therefore do not count SUSTech-SYSU as evidence that ranking transferred; that evidence rests on
APTOS, DDR and DeepDRiD.

Where the scores fell did not transfer, and it moved in opposite directions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:shift}b). On APTOS and SUSTech-SYSU the models scored images of the same true grade
higher than in development for at least three of the five grades (APTOS: \pn{r2.aptos.A1above}
of ten models; SUSTech-SYSU: \pn{r2.sustech.A1above} of ten); on DDR and DeepDRiD they scored them
lower (DDR: \pn{r2.ddr.A1below} of ten; DeepDRiD: \pn{r2.deepdrid.A1below} of ten; pre-specified
outcome A1 \pn{r2.A1.verdict}). The overall direction on APTOS and DDR was known when A1 was written; on DeepDRiD
and SUSTech-SYSU it was not, and the same conditional pattern appeared. The shift is therefore not a
change in prevalence alone, under which the score given the true grade would not move. Its shape differed between the two populations. On
APTOS it was concentrated in one grade: for the reference model, mild images had a mean expected
grade of \pn{r2.aptos.e08.es1} against \pn{r2.dev.e08.es1} in development, while no-DR images
scored lower (\pn{r2.aptos.e08.es0} against \pn{r2.dev.e08.es0}). On DDR every grade scored lower,
from \pn{r2.ddr.e08.es1} against \pn{r2.dev.e08.es1} for mild to \pn{r2.ddr.e08.es4} against
\pn{r2.dev.e08.es4} for proliferative DR. Over-grading on APTOS and under-grading on DDR followed:
the mean predicted-minus-true grade at the shipped rule was \pn{aptos.R1.mean.min} to
\pn{aptos.R1.mean.max} on APTOS and \pn{ddr.R1.mean.min} to \pn{ddr.R1.mean.max} on DDR.

A referral threshold fitted on development predictions therefore did not hold its operating
point (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}). Fitted for 85\% sensitivity, the reference model referred
\pn{aptos.tr.e08.B.sens}\% of referable patients on APTOS and \pn{sustech.tr.e08.B.sens}\% on
SUSTech-SYSU but \pn{ddr.tr.e08.B.sens}\% on DDR (pre-specified O8 \pn{ddr.o8}); only on DeepDRiD,
where the shift was smallest, did it land near its target (\pn{deepdrid.tr.e08.B.sens}\%).

Models fine-tuned on \pn{eye.eb3.trainn} EyePACS images instead of the development pool
reproduced the direction of the shift but not the sign of the threshold error (Supplementary
Table~S7). Like the development-pool models, they over-graded APTOS (mean predicted-minus-true grade
\pn{eye.dn.aptos.mean} for DenseNet121, \pn{eye.eb3.aptos.mean} for EfficientNet-B3) and
under-graded DDR (\pn{eye.dn.ddr.mean} and \pn{eye.eb3.ddr.mean}), with a referral area under the
curve of \pn{eye.auc.min}--\pn{eye.auc.max} on the six external sets. Yet a threshold fitted on their
held-out EyePACS fold for 85\% sensitivity over-referred on every set
(\pn{eye.tr85.min}--\pn{eye.tr85.max}\%; outcome E2 \pn{eye.E2.verdict}), DDR included
(\pn{eye.dn.ddr.tr85}\% and \pn{eye.eb3.ddr.tr85}\%, against \pn{ddr.tr.e08.B.sens}\% from the
development pool). A transferred threshold carries the reference standard of the set it was fitted
on. EyePACS referable images, each graded once, were harder to rank than those of any external set
(development area under the curve \pn{eye.dn.devauc} and \pn{eye.eb3.devauc}, against at least
\pn{eye.auc.min} externally), so the cut that catches 85\% of them is permissive elsewhere; the
adjudicated development pool gives a stricter cut, which under-refers where the models under-grade.
The same reference standard is the likely reason for the modest development QWK of the EyePACS models
(\pn{eye.dn.devqwk} and \pn{eye.eb3.devqwk}). The direction of the shift belonged to the target
population; the sign of a transferred threshold's error belonged to the development labels.

\subsection{Why the mapping moved}

Image quality can produce under-grading, but it does not explain DDR. Re-scored out of fold after
degradation, the development pool was under-graded by both re-scored models: JPEG compression at
quality 30 shifted the mean predicted-minus-true grade by \pn{mech.e08.jpeg.dmean} and
\pn{mech.e11full.jpeg.dmean} and referable sensitivity at the shipped rule by
\pn{mech.e08.jpeg.dsens} and \pn{mech.e11full.jpeg.dsens} points; Gaussian blur shifted the
reference model's mean grade by \pn{mech.e08.blur.dmean} and its sensitivity by
\pn{mech.e08.blur.dsens} points (outcome A2 \pn{mech.A2.verdict}; the undegraded re-scoring
reproduced the archived out-of-fold QWK, \pn{mech.e08.orig.qwk} against \pn{mech.e08.arch.qwk};
Supplementary Table~S8). DDR images, however, were not degraded on these measures: as used here they
share the 512-pixel size and the estimated JPEG quality of the Messidor-2 development images (\pn{stats.ddr.jpegq} against
\pn{stats.messidor2.jpegq}) and have higher contrast and Laplacian variance (\pn{stats.ddr.sharp}
against \pn{stats.messidor2.sharp}). Resolution, compression and blur therefore do not account for
the under-grading on DDR; camera and population characteristics we could not measure, or a stricter
grading convention, remain candidates that public data cannot separate. The APTOS pattern is of a
different kind: mild images alone moved up by about one grade while no-DR images moved down, which a
global change in image quality does not produce and a difference in how APTOS's single grader
assigned the mild grade would. Where that is the cause, local recalibration fits the local grading
standard (Discussion).

\subsection{What the decision rule can and cannot recover}

Where ranking survived, much of the external loss was a property of the rule. On APTOS every
development model lost the mild grade at the shipped rule (recall
\pn{aptos.R1.mild.min}--\pn{aptos.R1.mild.max}\%, against \pn{aptos.devmild.min}--\pn{aptos.devmild.max}\%
in development; Fig.~\ref{fig:rules}a), and so did all five seeds of the reference model
(\pn{seeds.aptos.mildR1.lo}--\pn{seeds.aptos.mildR1.hi}\%; O3 \pn{seeds.o3}). Cut-points refitted
on half of the external labels and applied to the other half (R4m) raised mild recall to
\pn{aptos.R4m.mild.min}--\pn{aptos.R4m.mild.max}\% with mild F1 of
\pn{r2.aptos.R4m.mildf1.min}--\pn{r2.aptos.R4m.mildf1.max} (precision \pn{r2.aptos.e11full.R4m.mildp}
for the selected model), above the development mild F1 of the same models under the same
macro-recall objective with cut-points cross-fitted across folds
(\pn{ph.dev.mildmr.min}--\pn{ph.dev.mildmr.max}; \pn{ph.dev.e11full.mildmr} for the selected
model). The gain was a trade, not a free repair: \pn{r2.aptos.e11full.R4m.modtomild}
moderate images were relabelled mild and referable sensitivity fell from
\pn{r2.aptos.e11full.R1.sens}\% to \pn{r2.aptos.e11full.R4m.sens}\%, because mild DR is not referable.
In the reverse direction the model trained on APTOS under-graded Messidor-2 (O6 \pn{rev.o6}) and
referred \pn{rev.m2.R1.sens}\% of referable eyes at its shipped rule while ranking them with an area
under the curve of \pn{rev.m2.auroc}; refitted cut-points raised sensitivity to \pn{rev.m2.R4.sens}\%.

Where ranking or the grade itself did not survive, the rule could not help. On DDR the mild grade
was not collapsed (recall \pn{ddr.R1.mild.min}--\pn{ddr.R1.mild.max}\%) and no rule raised its F1
above \pn{r2.ddr.R4m.mildf1.max}. The collapse of mild recall was specific to APTOS, but low mild F1
was not: even with cut-points refitted on external labels it stayed at or below
\pn{r2.deepdrid.R4m.mildf1.max} on DeepDRiD and \pn{r2.sustech.R4m.mildf1.max} on SUSTech-SYSU, as in
the released ORDER-DR predictions (below) and in a leave-one-domain-out benchmark of ordinal models
in which mild F1 never exceeded about 0.16 on any held-out domain\cite{gradeye2026}. In the reverse direction mild
recall at the shipped rule was \pn{rev.m2.R1.mild}\%, so the pre-specified predictions that a fine-tuned model would reproduce
the published mild-grade collapse (O4) and that refitted cut-points would triple it (O5) were
both falsified. RETFound ranked APTOS images as well as the convolutional models (area under the
curve \pn{rf.aptos.auroc}) and most of its QWK deficit there was the rule (\pn{rf.aptos.R1.qwk} at
its shipped rule, \pn{rf.aptos.R4.qwk} refitted), but on DDR its ranking itself degraded
(\pn{rf.ddr.auroc}; QWK \pn{rf.ddr.R4.qwk} even refitted).

Rules that need no external labels did not close the gap. Cut-points fitted on development data
(R2, R3) left APTOS mild recall at \pn{aptos.R2.mild.min}--\pn{aptos.R2.mild.max}\% and lowered
external QWK for \pn{aptos.r2worse} of 10 models. Label-free prior adaptation (R5) closed at
least a quarter of the DDR referral-sensitivity gap between R1 and R4 in only \pn{r2.ddr.B25} of
10 models (outcome B \pn{r2.B.verdict}). Because R5 decodes by argmax, its fair reference is the
argmax of the unadapted posteriors (R0a), which already differs from R1; against R0a, prior
adaptation changed referable sensitivity by a median of \pn{ph.aptos.r5.dsens.med} points on APTOS,
\pn{ph.ddr.r5.dsens.med} on DDR, \pn{ph.deepdrid.r5.dsens.med} on DeepDRiD and
\pn{ph.sustech.r5.dsens.med} on SUSTech-SYSU, and predicted any mild image in only
\pn{ph.r5.nmild.models} of \pn{ph.r5.n} model--population pairs (Supplementary Table~S11). That is
what a shift in the score given the grade, rather than in prevalence, should produce.

\subsection{Model comparisons depend on the decision rule}

The pre-specified prediction that the choice among deployable rules (R1--R3) would move mild
recall and referable sensitivity more than the choice among models (O7) was falsified on both
APTOS and DDR: models trained on the same data differed materially at their shipped rules
(median within-model range \pn{aptos.o7.mildrecall.within} against a between-model range of
\pn{aptos.o7.mildrecall.between} points of mild recall on APTOS, \pn{ddr.o7.refsens.within} against
\pn{ddr.o7.refsens.between} points of referable sensitivity on DDR). Which model looked best,
however, depended on the rule and on the metric. The rankings of models by external QWK at the
shipped rule and by referable sensitivity at a fixed 90\% specificity were at most weakly related
(Kendall $\tau$ \pn{ph.aptos.tau} on APTOS, \pn{ph.ddr.tau} on DDR, \pn{ph.deepdrid.tau} on DeepDRiD
and \pn{ph.sustech.tau} on SUSTech-SYSU; permutation $p$ \pn{ph.tau.p.min}--\pn{ph.tau.p.max}), so
with ten models neither ranking could be predicted from the other.

Under the matched decision rule several conclusions of the usual kind changed
(Table~\ref{tab:record}). On APTOS, the selected EfficientNet-B3 model led the reference DenseNet121 by
\pn{rec.bb.R1.qwk} QWK \pn{rec.bb.R1.qwkci} at the shipped rule and by \pn{rec.bb.R4.qwk}
\pn{rec.bb.R4.qwkci} with both models refitted, and per-grade differences changed sign between
operating points. On development data, linear-probe-then-fine-tune appeared to cost
\pn{rec.lpft.ship} accuracy points \pn{rec.lpft.shipci} at shipped cut-points and gained
\pn{rec.lpft.match} \pn{rec.lpft.matchci} at matched ones. The rule is not a one-way filter: the
development advantage of EfficientNet-B3 over its DenseNet121 control survived it
(\pn{iv.effnet.ship} shipped, \pn{iv.effnet.diff} \pn{iv.effnet.ci} matched).

The same held for an independently trained, published model family. Applying our rules to the
released image-level probabilities of ORDER-DR\cite{sheng2026orderdr} reproduced its reported
Messidor-2 result exactly (QWK \pn{odr.odr.R2.qwk} $\pm$ \pn{odr.odr.R2.qwksd}; Supplementary
Table~S6); with external cut-points all four released models converged to QWK
\pn{odr.R4.qwk.min}--\pn{odr.R4.qwk.max}, so the reported advantage of the fused model over its
best branch was a decision-rule effect, while mild F1 stayed at or below \pn{odr.R4m.f1.max}.

Training noise set the scale for all of these effects (O1). Matched development QWK varied with
a standard deviation of \pn{seeds.dev.sd} across five seeds of one configuration
(\pn{seeds.dev.lo}--\pn{seeds.dev.hi}). Between models that differed only in their seed,
\pn{seeds.aptos.sigR1} of \pn{seeds.aptos.ntests} per-grade recall comparisons on APTOS had a
bootstrap interval excluding zero at the shipped rule and \pn{seeds.aptos.sigR4} with refitted
cut-points (O2 \pn{seeds.o2}). An image-level bootstrap captures the sampling of test images, not
the variance of training, so per-grade ``effects'' of the size usually attributed to architecture
arise between identical architectures.

\subsection{Agreement statistics cannot see the referral operating point}

Holding the other cut-points at their QWK-optimal values and sweeping the referral cut
(Fig.~\ref{fig:frontier}), every operating point of the selected model within 0.01 of its maximum
QWK on DDR spanned referable sensitivity from \pn{ddr.band.e11full.sens.lo}\% to
\pn{ddr.band.e11full.sens.hi}\%: between \pn{ddr.band.e11full.miss.lo} and
\pn{ddr.band.e11full.miss.hi} of \pn{ddr.band.e11full.nref} referable patients missed, with QWK
unable to tell the operating points apart. In a screening programme with 10\% referable
prevalence, the selected model's shipped rule on DDR would miss \pn{r2.ddr.e11full.R1.miss10}
referable patients per 10\,000 screened (negative predictive value \pn{r2.ddr.e11full.R1.npv10}\%),
and cut-points refitted on local labels \pn{r2.ddr.e11full.R4.miss10}, at the cost of
\pn{r2.ddr.e11full.R4.fref10} rather than \pn{r2.ddr.e11full.R1.fref10} false referrals
(Supplementary Table~S9).

\subsection{How many local labels a referral threshold needs}

With the pre-specified objective --- a referral threshold set on $n$ locally labelled images for
85\% sensitivity --- the probability that sensitivity on the remaining images fell below 80\% with
200 images was \pn{r2.C.harm200.min}--\pn{r2.C.harm200.max}\% for the selected and reference models
on every external set (outcome C \pn{r2.C.verdict}), while mean sensitivity stayed close to target
at every size. Those sets are enriched, however
(\pn{ph.prev.min}--\pn{ph.prev.max}\% referable): 200 of their images contain about
\pn{ph.k200} referable cases, and the threshold depends on nothing else. Counted in referable cases,
the risk was nearly identical on every population and for both models and agreed with the
distribution-free closed form within \pn{ph.sim.maxdev} points (Fig.~\ref{fig:samplesize}a;
Supplementary Table~S10): \pn{ph.harm.k20}\% with 20 referable cases, \pn{ph.harm.k60}\% with 60,
\pn{ph.harm.k120}\% with 120 and \pn{ph.harm.k200}\% with 200. At a screening prevalence of 10\%,
200 consecutive images therefore carry a \pn{ph.harm.n200.p10}\% risk, and about 1\,000
(\pn{ph.harm.n1000.p10}\%) to 2\,000 images (\pn{ph.harm.n2000.p10}\%) are needed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:samplesize}b). Fewer cases suffice if the threshold is set more conservatively: with
60 referable cases, a cut at \pn{ph.req.k60.r05}\% sample sensitivity keeps the risk at or below
5\%, at a cost in specificity that depends on the population (for the selected model on DDR,
\pn{ph.ddr.e11full.spec90}\% rather than \pn{ph.ddr.e11full.spec85}\% at the thresholds giving
90\% and 85\% sensitivity).

\subsection{Interventions on the representation}

Of \pn{iv.n} pre-specified interventions on the model, \pn{iv.nfalsified} failed their
criterion under the matched decision rule (Supplementary Fig.~S1 and Table~S5). Only
the EfficientNet-B3 backbone improved development QWK; the retinal foundation model, fine-tuned at
its native 224 pixels, fell below a DenseNet121 at the same resolution by \pn{iv.retfound.diff}
\pn{iv.retfound.ci} over \pn{iv.retfound.folds} folds (O9 \pn{retfound.o9}). No intervention moved
the macular-oedema head, whose middle grade is supervised by \pn{dme.n1} images (Supplementary
Note~2).

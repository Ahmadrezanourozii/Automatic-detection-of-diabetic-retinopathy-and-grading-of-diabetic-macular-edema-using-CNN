\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

In the DR-grading settings studied here, an external failure separated into two components that
call for different remedies. The ranking of images by severity transferred to four new
populations and to models trained on twelve times more data; the mapping from score to grade did
not, and its error had opposite signs on different populations because the score given the true
grade moved. Where ranking survived, part of the loss was a property of the decision rule and
could be recovered without retraining --- the mild grade on APTOS, referral sensitivity in the
reverse direction --- but only as a trade between grades, and only with local labels. Where ranking
degraded, as for the foundation model on DDR, no rule helped. Two of our own predictions bound the
argument: deployable rules did not outweigh the choice of model, and the mild-grade collapse that
the literature reads as representational blindness was specific to one corpus. A reader who sees
an external failure should therefore ask, in order, whether the model still ranks the new
population, where its cut-points were set and on which population, and only then what it has
learned.

These results refine rather than contradict recent work. ORDER-DR separated threshold-dependent
grade assignment from threshold-independent risk ranking and noted threshold shift on external
data\cite{sheng2026orderdr}; reanalysing its released predictions shows that its reported
advantage over its own branches does not survive the matched decision rule. Tabibu and Abhyankar
found that the probability calibration of a binary referral model degrades across cameras mostly
through prevalence, and that 100--200 labelled images restore it\cite{tabibu2026discrimination}.
Our sample-size estimate agrees, for an ordinal model and a referral threshold rather than a
calibration map, but the mechanism we found differs: the score given the true grade moved, so
prior adaptation without labels failed, and temperature-style rescaling cannot be expected to help
either, as reported for frozen encoders\cite{poyrazer2026calibration}. Per-threshold calibration
has also been reported to degrade unevenly under domain shift\cite{gradeye2026}, consistent with
the grade-specific movement we observed on APTOS.

The central objection to this kind of analysis is that refitting cut-points on local labels may
fit local label noise rather than fix the model. The two populations answer it differently. On
DDR every grade moved down by a similar amount and degrading development images reproduced the
direction (A2), so recalibration there corrects a change in the images. On APTOS one grade moved
by about a whole step while its neighbours did not, which image quality does not produce and a
difference in how a single grader assigned the mild grade would; recalibration there aligns the
model with the local grading standard. Whether that is a fix depends on whether the local standard
is the one the screening programme acts on --- which, for a programme deploying a model, it
usually is. Either way, the rule must be fitted where it will be used, on a sample large enough
for its risk to be known.

The practical recommendations cost no additional training. External evaluations should report
discrimination at each ordinal threshold beside grade-level agreement, the full referral operating
curve, and the transfer gap of any development-fitted threshold, since the same threshold landed
\pn{aptos.tr.e08.A.gap} and \pn{ddr.tr.e08.A.gap} points from an 80\% target on two populations.
Model comparisons should be made under a matched decision rule that names its fitting procedure,
with differences judged against the variation between seeds of one configuration. Deployment
should include setting the referral threshold on a local sample: with about 200 labelled images
the probability of missing an 80\% sensitivity floor was below one in five on every population,
and with 400 it was a few per cent, in line with advice on updating prediction models for new
settings\cite{janssen2008updating,vergouwe2017updating,riley2021samplesize}.

The study has limitations. All data are public and retrospective, the populations are defined by
dataset rather than by a prospective screening programme, and APTOS and EyePACS labels come from
single graders. Patient identifiers are unavailable for most corpora, so resampling is by image
and fellow-eye pairs may be correlated. The external-label rules (R4, R4m) are diagnostics of what
local labels could recover, not external validation; the deployable recommendation rests on the
separate sample-size analysis. Referable disease was defined by DR grade alone because macular
oedema is not graded in the external sets, whereas real screening refers for both; the
macular-oedema head could not be validated externally and is reported only in the Supplement. The
reverse direction, the seeds and the large-data models each use one or two architectures. Finally,
the analysis plans were committed to version control rather than to a public registry, and the
pre-specification of development runs trained before the repository history begins cannot be
verified. The decision-rule effects should be confirmed prospectively, with cut-points fitted on
an initial local sample and then frozen.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

External failures and apparent differences between DR grading models are routinely attributed to
learned representations. In the settings studied here a substantial part of them lay in the
decision rule that converts scores into grades and could be diagnosed and repaired without
retraining, while the remainder reflected real losses of ranking that no rule recovers. Reporting
per-threshold discrimination, full operating curves, the transfer gap of thresholds and
matched-decision-rule comparisons, and setting referral thresholds on a local sample of known size
before deployment, would separate the two.

\abstract{Deep learning models that grade diabetic retinopathy lose performance on new
populations, and the losses are usually attributed to what the network has learned. We tested
whether the ordinal decision rule, not the representation, is responsible, in a pre-registered
study of models trained on \pn{n.dev} images and evaluated on three external directions
(APTOS, DDR, and APTOS to Messidor-2). Moving cut-points alone, without retraining, raised
external mild-retinopathy recall from \pn{aptos.R1.mild.min}--\pn{aptos.R1.mild.max}\% to
\pn{aptos.R4m.mild.min}--\pn{aptos.R4m.mild.max}\% on APTOS. Quadratic weighted kappa varied by
less than 0.01 across operating points spanning \pn{aptos.band.e11full.sens.lo}--\pn{aptos.band.e11full.sens.hi}\%
referral sensitivity. Models differing only by random seed produced spurious per-grade
differences, and refitting a published model family's cut-points erased its reported fusion
gain. Development-fitted thresholds did not transfer, while about 200 local labels sufficed to
recalibrate with \pn{recal.200.harm}\% risk of harm. Eight of nine interventions on the
representation failed. Grading models should be compared at matched calibration and
recalibrated locally.}

\begin{abstract}
Diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading models lose performance on new populations, and the losses are
usually attributed to the learned representation, although ordinal models convert scores into
grades with a rarely examined decision rule. Under analysis plans committed in advance to
version control, we evaluated ten development models on four external
populations (APTOS 2019, DDR, DeepDRiD and SUSTech-SYSU), a model trained on APTOS in the reverse
direction, two models trained on about 28\,000 EyePACS images, five seeds and a published model's released
predictions. We separated discrimination at each ordinal threshold
from the mapping of scores to grades, and compared models under a matched decision rule that refits
both models' cut-points identically. Ranking transferred everywhere
(area under the curve for referable DR at least \pn{r2.auc1.allmin}), but the score given the true
grade moved in opposite directions: the same models over-graded APTOS and under-graded DDR, so a
threshold fitted for 85\% development sensitivity reached \pn{aptos.tr.e08.B.sens}\% and
\pn{ddr.tr.e08.B.sens}\%. Refitting cut-points without retraining recovered the mild grade on APTOS
and raised referral sensitivity in the reverse direction from \pn{rev.m2.R1.sens}\% to
\pn{rev.m2.R4.sens}\%, but did not repair losses of ranking; label-free prior adaptation did
not help. Contrary to two of our predictions, models differed more than deployable rules, and the
mild-grade collapse was specific to one corpus.
About 200 locally labelled images set a referral threshold with less than a one-in-five risk of
missing an 80\% sensitivity floor. External evaluations should report per-threshold discrimination
and operating curves, and referral thresholds should be set locally before deployment.
\end{abstract}

\abstract{Diabetic retinopathy grading models lose performance on new populations, and the
losses are usually attributed to the learned representation. In a
pre-registered study of ten development models evaluated on APTOS and DDR, five seed replicates
and a reverse-direction model, we separated that contribution from the ordinal decision rule
that converts scores into grades. Discrimination of referable disease transferred (area under
the curve \pn{aptos.auroc.min}--\pn{aptos.auroc.max}), but the models over-graded APTOS and
under-graded DDR: a threshold fitted for 85\% development sensitivity reached
\pn{aptos.tr.e08.B.sens}\% and \pn{ddr.tr.e08.B.sens}\%. Refitting cut-points alone, without
retraining, restored APTOS mild-grade recall from \pn{aptos.R1.mild.min}--\pn{aptos.R1.mild.max}\%
to \pn{aptos.R4m.mild.min}--\pn{aptos.R4m.mild.max}\% and raised reverse-direction referral
sensitivity from \pn{rev.m2.R1.sens}\% to \pn{rev.m2.R4.sens}\%. Contrary to our pre-registered
predictions, model choice mattered more than development-fitted rules, and the mild-grade
collapse did not generalise.
Quadratic weighted kappa varied by under 0.01 across DDR operating points spanning
\pn{ddr.band.e11full.sens.lo}--\pn{ddr.band.e11full.sens.hi}\% referral sensitivity. External
evaluations should report operating curves; deployment should include local recalibration.}
